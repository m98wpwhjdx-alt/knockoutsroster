DAS Airway Decision Aid

Clinical use: educational aid for theatre/ICU briefing and viva preparation. It is not an official DAS product and does not replace current DAS algorithms, senior judgement, or local difficult-airway policy.

Preparation and planning before tracheal intubation

Airway assessment

  • Review previous airway history and bedside predictors of difficulty.
  • Assess the cricothyroid membrane as part of the airway exam.
  • Assess and manage pulmonary aspiration risk.
  • Assess physiological difficulty: oxygen reserve, haemodynamics, acidosis, RV failure, sepsis, pregnancy, obesity.
  • Review relevant imaging when available.
  • Consider awake airway visualisation: nasendoscopy or awake videolaryngoscopy.

Decision trigger

Is difficulty anticipated?
Is awake tracheal intubation appropriate?

If difficulty is anticipated and ATI is appropriate: perform awake tracheal intubation. If ATI is not appropriate or difficulty is not anticipated, proceed only after asleep-intubation preparation is complete.

ATI

DAS awake tracheal intubation: high-yield rules

1

Indication threshold

Consider ATI when predictors of difficult airway management are present, especially when difficulty is expected in intubation, facemask ventilation, SAD ventilation, aspiration management, apnoea tolerance, or eFONA.

2

sTOP principle

Sedation, Topicalisation, Oxygenation, Performance. Topicalisation is the foundation; sedation must not compensate for poor topical anaesthesia.

3

Confirmation before induction

Only induce anaesthesia after tracheal tube position is confirmed by a two-point check: visual confirmation and waveform capnography.

Mandatory ATI safeguards

  • Use a cognitive aid/checklist.
  • Give supplemental oxygen throughout.
  • Establish and test effective topicalisation.
  • Lidocaine maximum: 9 mg/kg lean body weight.
  • Limit attempts: 3 + 1 by a more experienced operator.

Abort / change strategy if

  • Patient cooperation is lost.
  • Bleeding or secretions prevent safe visualisation.
  • Oxygenation worsens despite optimisation.
  • Repeated attempts risk airway trauma or complete obstruction.
  • Tube position cannot be confirmed.
A–D

DAS 2025 adult unanticipated difficult tracheal intubation algorithm

If difficulty is experienced, call for help. Maintain oxygenation, use waveform capnography, limit attempts, communicate clearly, and progress through the declared plans.

Plan A
Tracheal intubation

Goal: successful tracheal intubation — maximum 3 + 1 attempts

Peroxygenation: facemask ± nasal oxygen Use a videolaryngoscope Adequate neuromuscular blockade Time awareness
  • If unsuccessful, optimise: introducer such as bougie/stylet, external laryngeal manipulation, remove cricoid force if applied, improve position, change blade/device, change operator.
  • Airway assistant: monitor attempts/time, prompt equipment, prepare SAD for Plan B.
If unsuccessful: declare failed intubation; ensure eFONA kit immediately accessible; move to Plan B.
Verify success with two-point check:
1. Waveform capnography
2. Visual confirmation
Plan B
Supraglottic airway device

Goal: restore oxygenation/ventilation via SAD — maximum 3 attempts

Second-generation SAD Adequate neuromuscular blockade Consider size/device change
  • If ventilation is confirmed: stop, think, communicate.
  • Consider waking the patient, intubating through the SAD, proceeding without intubation, or FONA if indicated.
  • Blind tracheal intubation through an SAD is not recommended.
If unsuccessful: declare failed SAD ventilation; open eFONA kit; move to Plan C.
Confirm ventilation with waveform capnography
Plan C
Final facemask ventilation

Goal: final attempt at facemask ventilation

Deliver 100% oxygen Adequate neuromuscular blockade Optimise position OP/NP airway adjuncts Two-person technique Adequate depth of anaesthesia
  • Ensure help is present or on the way.
  • Airway assistant opens the eFONA kit and assists facemask ventilation.
  • If ventilation is confirmed, senior decision: wake/reverse or proceed to FONA.
If unsuccessful: declare cannot intubate, cannot oxygenate; move immediately to Plan D.
Confirm ventilation with waveform capnography
Plan D
Emergency FONA

Goal: emergency front-of-neck airway

Continue 100% oxygen to upper airway Scalpel size 10 Bougie 6.0 mm cuffed tube
  • Maximal neck extension if feasible.
  • Full neuromuscular blockade.
  • Suction available.
  • If unsuccessful: smaller tube, extend incision, change position and/or operator.
  • Assistant: provide kit, load tube onto bougie, remove bougie, inflate cuff, connect circuit and capnography.
Confirm ventilation with waveform capnography

Awake tracheal intubation

Use when difficulty is anticipated and ATI is appropriate. Oxygen throughout, topicalisation tested, lidocaine ≤ 9 mg/kg lean body weight, minimal sedation, attempts 3 + 1, induce only after visual + capnographic confirmation.

Plan A

Use videolaryngoscopy, optimise first-pass success, ensure NMB and time awareness. After 3 + 1 failed attempts or earlier if no improvement is possible, declare failed intubation and move to Plan B.

Plan B

Insert second-generation SAD. Confirm ventilation by waveform capnography. Stop, think, communicate: wake, intubate via SAD, proceed with SAD, or FONA. If failed SAD ventilation, open eFONA kit and move to Plan C.

Plan C

Final facemask ventilation attempt: 100% oxygen, optimise position, adjuncts, two-person technique, adequate NMB and depth. If not oxygenating, declare CICO and move to Plan D.

Plan D

Emergency front-of-neck airway: scalpel-bougie-tube with 6.0 cuffed tube. Continue oxygen delivery, ensure help present, and confirm ventilation by waveform capnography.

Trap questions and safe answers

“Why not just induce?”

Because induction can remove airway tone, spontaneous ventilation, and rescue options. If difficulty is anticipated across intubation, ventilation, aspiration, oxygen reserve, or eFONA, ATI should be considered.

“Can you deepen sedation if the patient coughs?”

No. Stop, oxygenate, improve topicalisation within safe lidocaine dosing, reassess, then proceed. Sedation is not a substitute for topical anaesthesia.

“How many attempts?”

ATI and Plan A intubation both use a 3 + 1 concept: three attempts, then one more by a more experienced operator, unless earlier progression is safer.

“What proves the tube is in?”

Waveform capnography is central. For ATI, use two-point confirmation: visual confirmation plus waveform capnography before induction.

“SAD rescue successful — now what?”

Stop, think, communicate. Options include waking the patient, intubating via SAD, proceeding with SAD if appropriate, or FONA if tracheal intubation is essential or risk is unacceptable.

“When do you declare CICO?”

After failed tracheal intubation, failed SAD ventilation, and unsuccessful final facemask oxygenation. Declare clearly and proceed immediately to eFONA.
